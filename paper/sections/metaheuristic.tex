\section{Hybrid metaheuristic baseline}
\label{sec:metaheuristic}

To situate the matheuristics against a strong \emph{pure}
metaheuristic baseline, one that never calls a MIP solver, we developed
a hybrid procedure (\ILS) combining components with strong track
records in the GRASP literature. We summarize it here.

Solutions are schedules $s\in(\{0\}\cup J)^{|T|}$ evaluated by the
same exact evaluator used by the matheuristics
(Section~\ref{sec:matheuristics}), so all methods optimize literally
the same objective. Construction is a reactive GRASP
\citep{feo1995greedy,prais2000reactive}: candidate processes for each
period are ranked by myopic shortage reduction, the restricted
candidate list parameter $\alpha$ being drawn from a pool whose
probabilities adapt to observed solution quality. Local search is a
variable neighborhood descent over process-exchange and Or-opt-style
relocation moves on the schedule; for long horizons, moves are
restricted by a sliding time window with a tabu mechanism to keep
iteration costs bounded. The improvement loop is an iterated local
search \citep{lourenco2010iterated} whose perturbation applies
multi-bridge reinsertions of schedule segments whose strength adapts
to the horizon length. Each of ten workers, run in parallel from
different seeds, maintains its own elite pool and applies path
relinking within it
\citep{laguna1999grasp,resende2016optimization}. The original run
summaries also contained a final cross-run relinking stage applied
after the ten workers had finished. Because that additional computation
was not charged to the 3,600-second limit, its results are excluded
from every equal-cutoff comparison and are retained only as a
post-analysis diagnostic. The incumbent trajectory of every worker is
logged.
Parameter values follow the released implementation: reactive
$\alpha$ drawn from $\{0, 0.05, \ldots, 0.30\}$ with exponentially
smoothed weights, randomized tabu tenure in $[5,12]$ with aspiration,
windowed tabu search for $|T|>40$, Or-opt blocks of one to three
periods, elite pools of ten solutions, and intra-run relinking every
four improvements.

Under the protocol of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, \ILS{} occupies a
deliberately generous position in terms of stochastic attempts: its
per-instance result is the best incumbent attained by ten workers
within the stated cutoff. Thus the comparison credits \ILS{} with more
opportunities to find a good incumbent, but not with the uncharged
cross-run post-processing stage. Historically, this procedure is the
modernized descendant of the heuristics proposed for the PSP in the
original studies \citep{luche2009combining}. This implementation serves
as the strong hybrid metaheuristic baseline in the computational study.
