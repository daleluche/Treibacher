\begin{abstract}
We revisit a real-world process selection and lot-sizing problem
from the electrofused grains industry, in which each production
process yields multiple products in fixed proportions and at most
one process runs per period. The plant's real order book was solved
to optimality by the original study, but its enlarged benchmark
instances motivated heuristic approaches. Using comparison criteria
fixed before the corresponding experiments were run, we compare
a modern MIP solver on
the monolithic model, temporal-decomposition matheuristics
(relax-and-fix construction with fix-and-optimize improvement, and
a warm-started monolith), and a strong hybrid GRASP-ILS baseline
with intra-run path relinking, on a benchmark of 60 derived
instances spanning horizons from 19 to 190 periods. We also
isolate a modeling effect that governs difficulty: penalizing
accumulated excess production at a thousandth of the weight of
shortage moves four instance sets from routinely closed to largely
open, holding everything else fixed. The pure metaheuristic,
on the scales where it was run, is dominated by MIP-based methods
even when credited with the best of ten concurrent attempts. The
resulting method-choice map has two axes:
In the tested benchmark families, the monolithic MIP achieved most
per-instance wins through 5X, whereas decomposition achieved the
majority at 8X and 10X under the 3{,}600-second cutoff. Tripling the
monolithic MIP cutoff restored its lead on seven of the ten largest
benchmark instances, while one-hour decomposition remained superior
on three. The supporting material includes solutions, bounds, logged
trajectories, and per-window logs, including
dual signals, to support reproduction and learning-based extensions.
\end{abstract}
